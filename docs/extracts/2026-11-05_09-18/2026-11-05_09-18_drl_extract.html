1. The whole idea

In one line. A neural network looks at what one automated vehicle knows, picks a target headway for it, and is nudged after each batch of driving so that choices which went better than expected become more likely. Nothing else is learned: no road model, no physics, no rule.

16 → 4numbers in → probabilities out
5,508numbers in the policy
1 knobtau, the target headway. Not speed, not lane, not the ramp
10 show often one vehicle may change its mind
38 Mdecisions seen per policy in the DRL shot, 500 updates
3.6 hwall time per policy in the shot, 7 at once

Why it is called deep and reinforcement

Deep: the policy is a neural network, here small, 2 layers of 64. Reinforcement: nobody shows it correct answers. It only gets a score after acting, so it must try things and learn from the score.

Where the hope for an edge comes from

A fixed rule gives the same headway whatever is happening. The policy sees speed, gaps and position, so it can react. That is worth something only where the best headway changes with conditions it can see. Sections 12 and 13 show where that holds on this plant.

What it cannot do

It cannot brake, steer, change lane or hold the ramp. It cannot act when the vehicle ahead is not an ALKS. It knows nothing about the future and has no road model. Section 4 shows how much of the time its action has any effect.

2. Five clocks

In one line. Everything runs on five clocks. Most confusion about this project's plant and reward comes from which clock a thing is on, as V367 showed.

Why this matters. The reward reads braking only on the whole-second ticks. Between two ticks there are 10 simulation steps, and a short hard braking can start and end inside them. That is how P0 hid 9 m/s² braking from every 1-Hz count (V367). The fix made the plant itself gentler; it did not make the reward faster.

One update is 3 episodes side by side, one each at 4,090, 4,600 and 5,300 veh/h. Episodes hold many vehicles, so an update holds tens of thousands of decisions even though one vehicle decides only about 30-70 times in its trip. Measured on the shot, C*_updates.jsonl: decisions per update 69,000-88,000.

It sees present states only

No time, demand or memory. Two identical situations get the same answer whether the slowdown has lasted 10 s or 100 s. It can only infer a slowdown through low speed in its own bin or the merge zone.

Merge 2 has less coverage

Input [13] covers merge 1 only: in the code, roads 3a and the ends of 2 and 3 are the merge zone, while 4a, 4 and 5 are all "downstream". Merge 2 reaches the policy only through x, [12], [14] and [15].

What the reward does not contain

No fuel, no comfort, no jerk, no door-to-door delay of the vehicle itself, no queue on the ramp except through the shared count. A metric outside the reward can only improve by accident. The protocol reports delay and fuel anyway.

Credit: who earned what

33 % of the reward's mean magnitude is shared when steady, 45 % in X1 (trace), and one vehicle among 200-300 barely moves it. Before the per-vehicle terms worked, every vehicle got the same score (V294), so a good action and a bad one looked alike. Section 10.

The reward and the winning metric overlap

TTC below 3 s is in the reward and is also the metric on which confirmatory 2 was won. Rules are not trained on it. It is a surrogate, and V377 found it mostly marks a slow queue around merge 2, with a median follower speed of 5-7 m/s. So the protocol gates the word "safety".

A real bug, kept as a lesson (V294). A rewrite dropped the call that computed the per-vehicle terms. It only showed in training, where report_net is off. Every check ran with it on, so all checks passed while all per-CAV runs trained on the shared term alone, and explanations were written for behaviour that had this cause. Now the training path calls _net_stats_gap, and smoke tests run in training mode. With the intended reward, the policies then learned fast and consistently in the first 84 updates (V295).

6. The networks

In one line. Each network is a stack of simple formulas with adjustable numbers. The actor turns 16 inputs into 4 scores; softmax turns scores into probabilities. Training only changes the numbers.

Code: _mlp and Net, v5b/fleet/c_train.py:34-48. Count for the actor: (16×64+64) + (64×64+64) + (64×4+4) = 1,088 + 4,160 + 260 = 5,508. The stored policy reports the same. Only 8 of each layer's circles are drawn. No layer is shared between actor and critic.

The 4 scores are called logits. Only their differences matter. Softmax turns them into probabilities: raise e to each score, divide by the total. Entropy measures how undecided the result is: 1.386 for 4 equal choices, 0 for a certain one. e^entropy reads as "how many choices it is really considering".

Try it: from 4 scores to a choice

Training: stochastic

Each decision is drawn at random from the probabilities (_act, c_train.py:94). The vehicle therefore tries other levels, and the critic and advantage can judge them. This is the exploration.

Use: greedy

Each decision takes the largest probability (Greedy, c_train.py:51). All evaluation runs are greedy, so behaviour in evaluation differs from behaviour in training. That is one reason the training reward does not predict the evaluation result.

Why so small

My reading: small networks train faster and are less able to memorise the training seeds. This one is about 11,000 numbers in total. Whether a larger one would find more was not the question of confirmatory 3: its budget and architecture were fixed before data.

7. Value and advantage

In one line. Reward says how the last second went. The policy needs to know how a choice went, and its effects arrive later. The critic bridges that gap by predicting the future reward of a state.

Value: V(state) = the critic's guess of r(now) + γ r(next) + γ² r(next+1) + ... Surprise: δ(t) = r(t) + γ V(t+1) - V(t), better or worse than expected, one step Advantage: A(t) = δ(t) + γλ δ(t+1) + (γλ)² δ(t+2) + ..., surprises blended over later steps (GAE) Return target: G(t) = A(t) + V(t), what the critic is trained to predict

Code: gae, v5b/fleet/c_train.py:69. It runs backward through a vehicle's trip: last = r[t] + gamma*next_v - v[t] + gamma*lam*last. One step is one 10-s decision, and r is that interval's mean reward per second, so a steady reward r gives V = r / (1-γ), about 15 times r at γ 0.933.

Try it: how far ahead does the policy look?

Both settings in the buttons look about 150 s ahead, which is roughly the time to cross a merge area and feel the result: V309 corrected an earlier, wrong reading of 25 s. My reasoning, not measured: too short and a vehicle cannot connect a gap it opens now with a merge that goes smoothly a minute later. Too long and the estimate is mostly noise, because 200 other vehicles decide what happens in between.

Why λ exists

λ near 0 trusts the critic after one step: low noise, but biased if the critic is wrong. λ near 1 uses the real rewards all the way: unbiased, but noisy. 0.967 per 10 s here is close to the 0.9 per 30 s of the code's default, c_train.py:29.

Why the critic uses a fixed scale

Returns are divided by a scale fixed once at update 0, so the critic predicts numbers near 1. It comes from the first batch and differed from 1.39 to 3.40 across the 7 shot learners. Section 11 shows what that does to the value-loss curve.

What happens when a vehicle leaves

A vehicle that leaves the road or reaches the episode's end is cut, not ended: the critic's value stands in for what would follow (c_train.py:144, :158). So leaving the network is not treated as the end of the reward.

9. One PPO update

In one line. PPO asks: given what just happened, how should the probabilities move, without moving them so far that the batch stops being a fair guide?

ratio = new probability of the action taken / old probability clip term = -mean(min(ratio × A, clamp(ratio, 1-ε, 1+ε) × A)), ε = 0.2 value term = mean((V(state) - return target/scale)²) loss = clip term + 0.5 × value term - 0.01 × entropy

Code: update, v5b/fleet/c_train.py:177. Why clipped: the batch was collected with the old policy, so it says little about a much different one. With positive A, there is no gain from pushing the ratio above 1+ε. With negative A, no gain from pushing it below 1-ε.

10. One brain, many cars

In one line. There are hundreds of agents but only one set of numbers. Each vehicle uses the same policy with its own inputs, and all their experience trains it. Textbook names: parameter sharing, and centralised training with decentralised execution.

Why share

A vehicle only lives about 30-70 decisions. With separate policies each would have almost no data and the policy would die with the vehicle. Sharing gives 38 million decisions to one set of numbers.

The price of sharing

Vehicles in the same situation act the same way, apart from their random draws. Greedy use removes the draws, so two neighbours with similar inputs pick the same level. It cannot assign one vehicle to open a gap and the next to close it, unless their inputs differ.

The credit problem

The shared terms are the same for all. If 250 vehicles act, each one's effect on them is about 1/250 and is buried in noise. That is why the reward has per-vehicle terms, including the follower's TTC and braking. Before those worked, learning was poor (V294).

A moving target

Each vehicle's surroundings are other vehicles run by the same policy, which changes every update. What was a good choice can stop being so. The clip and the fresh batch per update limit this but do not remove it.

Why a road-wide critic helps

The critic only predicts. Giving it road-wide state, such as the zone speeds and queue, lets it explain away what the vehicle could not control, which leaves a cleaner advantage for the actor. The actor stays deployable, because it needs only what a vehicle knows. This is my reading of the method; the effect on this plant was not measured.

Staggered decisions

Vehicles have their own 10-s clocks, aligned to the 5-s environment steps. So at each step about half of them are asked (due, b_env.py:880). V290 introduced this after a run with 5-s decisions and a 30-s limit gave near-uniform probabilities. Its explanation was withdrawn in V294, because the reward was broken at the time; the per-vehicle clock stayed.

11. Reading training

In one line. Four curves tell you if training is alive. They do not tell you if the result is good. These come from the DRL shot: 7 learners, 500 updates, r2m3 on the earlier plant P0, about 3.6 h each. Confirmatory 3 will produce the same files on plant_c3.

Grey: each learner. Blue: their mean. Points are means over 10 updates, except the first, which is update 0 alone. Hover for values.

Reward: flat is not dead

It climbs from about -0.21 to -0.18 in the first 50-100 updates, then stays near -0.17 (V364). Noise per update is about 0.08, because each update draws new seeds and slowdowns. So reward here cannot prove learning after update 100, and it cannot compare runs on different plants: references, weights and vehicle counts all differ.

Entropy: the real sign of learning

From about 1.38, nearly uniform, to about 0.7 by update 150. e^0.7 is about 2, so the policy now hesitates between about 2 levels on average. Falling means it commits. A collapse to near 0 early would mean it stopped exploring, which did not happen.

Value loss: read with care

The logged curves look very different across learners, 0.15 to 0.83. Multiply by scale² and they match: about 1.6-1.9 by the end, down from about 2.6-3.3. So the difference was only units. The critic's typical error is about √1.7 ≈ 1.3, on values near -8 in the traced trips. Scale matters beyond units: the gradient limit applies to actor and critic together, so a larger value term can shrink the actor's step. Not measured.

What these curves cannot say

None of them measures how the policy does against a rule. That needs fresh seeds, greedy use and paired comparison. V297 to V298 is the warning: a policy met the bar at 5,300, then failed on fresh seeds.

15. How it will be judged

In one line. 5 fresh policies, tuned rules chosen on separate seeds, fixed margins, and a sentence for each outcome, all written before the data.

Shortened here. The full text and the extra limits are in docs/research/protocols/confirmatory_3.md, sections Claim rules and Sentences allowed. The word "safety" needs one more check on samples at 3 m/s² or harder. Otherwise the sentence says "fewer seconds at TTC below 3 s".

What to look at first when it ends

1. decide3_table.csv: did at least 4 of 5 clear? 2. Which opponents counted, and did only p333 count? 3. The copy rule: if it matches the policies, the reacting part adds nothing. 4. Map cells: where are policies clearly worse?

Three ways to read the outcome

Won on TTC: an advantage of reacting, on a surrogate, on an uncalibrated plant. Replication only: the old edge held against 0.8 s, not against tuned rules. Neither: the edge did not survive the fixed plant, and the line closes.

Why a loss is still a result

A pre-registered negative on the fixed plant, with tuned opponents and a logged path, is usable thesis material. V368's edge could still have been real on P0 and yet belong to what P0 hid.

Check yourself

Why does the policy mostly choose 0.8 s, even though nothing tells it to?

The flow term pays each vehicle for short spacing, directly from its own action. At motorway speed, following at 0.8 s rather than 1.6 s earns about +0.24 per second. Nothing else responds so directly. It holds 0.8 s until a stronger term, such as the -10 TTC or braking flags, pushes back.

A policy picks level 3 while its leader is a human car. What happens?

The target is remembered, and the level may still move, but the gate is closed, so tau stays 1.6 s. Once it has the same ALKS leader on two updates, tau falls to the level's value at once.

Reward stays flat from update 100 to 500. Has it stopped learning?

Not necessarily. Entropy still falls a little, moves and reversals drop, and the reward's noise (about 0.08 per update) can hide small gains. But it also shows no large gain is coming. Only evaluation can say how good it is.

Why can't you compare a reward from P0 and a reward from plant_c3?

The shared count term is relative to a no-control run on that plant, and other terms change with the plant's traffic. The same number means different things.

Why does X1 show strongly negative advantages for a trip that looks sensible?

This stored critic was trained on P0, not plant_c3, and it expects better than happens there. Advantage measures against the critic, so a wrong critic gives a wrong sign of surprise. In training the critic would adapt; here it is frozen.

If the copy rule matches the policies, what have you learned?

That the policies' value is in where they use which headway, which a fixed table can reproduce. The reacting part, using speed in real time, would not be adding anything measurable on those cells.

Why are 5 policies needed, and why at least 4 of 5?

One policy can win by luck of its random start. Requiring most of them to clear the bar tests the method and not one draw. No checkpoint or policy is selected.

What would change if the reward were sampled every 0.1 s?

Short hard brakings would cost what they should. It would be 10 times as many reward reads, and P0's hidden braking would probably have been paid for. It was not tested.
