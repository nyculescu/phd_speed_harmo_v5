\section{The reading: controllers, measures and the protocol fixed before data}\label{sec:the_reading}

This section defines every arm, measure and reading before any result, so that Section~\ref{sec:results} only fills them in.
The study ran as a sequence of readings on changing plants and policy sets: phase~1, confirmatory~2, confirmatory~3 and its replication 3b, round~1 and round~2.
Only round~2 decides; the earlier readings are reported as history and support no claim.
Round~2's protocol (Appendix~\ref{sec:supplement}) asks three questions:
Q1, does a new candidate policy beat round~1's winner (v3w) on fresh seeds, and does it then win against the rules;
Q2, does v3w still win against the rules when a rule tuned by CMA-ES for the cell is among them;
Q3 (report-only), how does close-following under each policy compare with no control across 4,500--6,000\,veh/h.

\subsection{Controllers}\label{sec:controllers}

Table~\ref{tab:controllers} lists the arms.
\emph{No control} leaves every ALKS at 1.6\,s.
\emph{p333} commands 0.8\,s to every ALKS that the V2V gate admits.

\emph{The grid} is confirmatory~3's set of tuned rules: six forms with 69 rules in total (uniform, 4; by zone, 30; on road 4a, 3; local adaptive, 12; adaptive on 4a, 12; own state, 8), tuned per primary cell on seeds 7,440,160--179 \ledger{V376}.
A seventh form, the copy rule, reproduces the stored baseline-v3 policies' choices, traced on seeds 7,440,180--189, which makes 70 rules.
On evaluation seeds the grid enters as 12 rule arms, p333 and the copy rule included, so p333 is not counted twice.

\emph{3E} rules are own-state rules (each ALKS's level depends only on what it measures itself) with four parameters in each of five zones, the zone edges lying at $-100$, 594.72, 1,389.02 and 2,083.74\,m from the start of 3a.
Their 20 parameters were searched with diagonal CMA-ES~\autocite{Hansen2001,Ros2008}\verify\ (initial step 0.25, population 24, generator seed 20261106).
Two variants enter round~2.
\emph{3E-T} (stored as \texttt{g032i20}) was tuned under the random training slowdown (condition~T, outside the plant's stated intended use) at 4,500--6,000\,veh/h on seeds 7,440,700--739, against the rule \texttt{os1\_15\_10} with a delay cap of +2\,\% relative to it; it ran 40 of 100 planned generations and its convergence was not checked \ledger{V443}.
We always name it ``a rule tuned by CMA-ES under the training slowdowns, not for this cell''.
\emph{3E-X1} (\texttt{g003i02}) and \emph{3E-X2} (\texttt{g016i21}) were each tuned for one decision cell at 5,300\,veh/h on seeds 7,440,740--779, 16 seeds and 40 generations, starting from \texttt{os0\_10\_10} (X1) and \texttt{os1\_15\_10} (X2), with p333 as reference and a hinge penalty on delay above +1\,\%.
A searched rule was admitted only if its delay was at most 2\,\% above p333's and its collisions at most p333's plus one.
The search ran on a rented server from a frozen commit \ledger{V461}.
We name each ``a rule tuned by CMA-ES for this cell on separate seeds''.

\emph{The policies} are per-CAV PPO~\autocite{Schulman2017}\verify\ headway policies: one shared policy decides for each ALKS from its own inputs.
Each recipe trains 5 learners, each with 3 environments and 500 updates, cycling through seven training levels from 4,500 to 6,000\,veh/h under the random training slowdown, on training seeds 7,464,000--7,471,799 shared by all candidates (common random numbers), with discount 0.933 and GAE parameter 0.967.
Inputs are normalised by running statistics \ledger{V388}.
The judged 1-Hz close-following count also enters the reward, and we disclose this because it means the policies were trained on the measure they are judged on.
For each learner the checkpoint (after 250 or 500 updates) is chosen on validation seeds only.
The arms are v3w, round~1's winner, stored as baseline-v4 before round~2's validation \ledger{V455}, and at most two round-2 finalists selected on validation (Appendix~\ref{sec:supplement}, Table~T17).

The rules know the condition and the demand level and act on a 5-s grid; the policies know neither, act every 10\,s, and choose among four levels.

\subsection{Cells and measures}\label{sec:cells_measures}

The decision cells are X1 and X2 at 5,300\,veh/h.
The band (4,500, 5,000, 5,250, 5,500 and 6,000\,veh/h under the random training slowdown, 20 seeds per level) is report-only and outside the plant's stated intended use.
History cells come from confirmatory~3's map for the baseline-v3 policies only; H1 and H2 among them are outside the intended use.

\emph{Close-following} is the number of follower-seconds with a closing rear TTC below 3\,s, sampled at 1\,Hz (L19).
The 3-s threshold was chosen for training \ledger{V265} and has no literature source.
TTC below 1.5 and 2\,s and severe samples, those needing at least 3\,m/s$^2$ of deceleration, are reported beside it.
\emph{All-vehicle delay} is the vehicle-seconds spent in the system minus distance over free-flow speed, counting unfinished vehicles to the window end and vehicles still waiting to enter (L18).
Finishers' delay, which leaves out both, is labelled separately wherever it appears.
We also report served flow; braking at 3 and 7\,m/s$^2$, counted every 0.1\,s; moving full-emergency events; and collisions.
The remaining counters (fuel, jerk, undertakes, lane-end stops, ramp delays, splits by road, speed and period) are in Table~T15 of the supplement.

\subsection{Statistic and sign convention}\label{sec:statistic}

For a policy arm $P$ and an opponent $O$ over paired seeds, the effect is
\begin{equation}
E = s \cdot \frac{\operatorname{median}(P - O)}{\operatorname{median}(O)} \times 100,
\end{equation}
with $s = -1$ for measures where less is better (delay, close-following, braking) and $s = +1$ for flow, so that a positive $E$ always means the policy arm is better.
Confidence intervals are percentile bootstraps of the paired differences~\autocite{Efron1993}\verify, 20,000 resamples, with the denominator held fixed and generator seed 20261104.
A policy arm's value on a seed is the mean over its 5 learners; the intervals therefore cover variation over evaluation seeds, not over training runs.
Every number in the paper uses this one convention, and every stored value in another convention is converted (Table~T18).
On an earlier line of this project, the choice of convention alone changed a reported range from 16--41\,\% to 10--35\,\% \ledger{V99}.

\subsection{Multiplicity}\label{sec:multiplicity}

Validation selects candidates and runs at 95\,\% with no correction; across its 14 tests, at least one passes by chance up to about 30\,\% of the time.
Reading T1 (a finalist against v3w, by either of two paths) uses a Bonferroni correction over $m$ = finalists $\times$ 2 cells.
Reading T2 uses $m$ = (finalists + v3w) $\times$ 2, which gives 97.5, 98.75 or 99.17\,\% intervals for 0, 1 or 2 finalists.
Adding 3E opponents does not change $m$, because T2 requires a win against every counting opponent.
Harm is read at 95\,\%, since a corrected interval would hide it, and map tiles use 95\,\% with no correction, which is why they decide nothing.

\subsection{Readings}\label{sec:readings}

Table~\ref{tab:readings} collects the thresholds.

\emph{Counting.} An opponent counts in a cell only if its delay is at most 2\,\% above p333's, on the point estimate.
This filter can drop a rule that has less close-following than the rules that count.

\emph{T2, the tuned reading.} A policy set wins a cell if at least 4 of its 5 policies, against every counting opponent, have at least 10\,\% less close-following with the interval excluding 0 and at least 20 fewer close-following seconds per run.
They must also stay within the side limits: delay at most 2\,\% worse, flow at most 0.5\,\% worse, braking at 3\,m/s$^2$ at most 5\,\% worse, full-emergency events at most 5\,\% or 5 per run worse, collisions at most the opponent's plus one; and, against p333, delay within 5\,\% and flow within 1\,\%.
A 5\,\% non-inferiority bound on delay is reported beside T2, because the 2\,\% point rule passes a policy that is truly 2\,\% worse about half the time (52\,\% in a resampling check, \ledger{V435}).

\emph{T1 and the advance rule.} A finalist is better than v3w in a cell by path (a), less delay with the interval excluding 0 and close-following at most 10\,\% higher, or by path (b), at least 10\,\% less close-following with the interval excluding 0 and delay at most 2\,\% higher.
Harm against v3w, more than 2\,\% worse delay or more than 10\,\% more close-following with the interval's upper bound below 0, removes a candidate.
A candidate is also removed (floor KILL) if, in either cell, its policies have less than 10\,\% fewer close-following seconds than p333, or more delay than no control with the interval excluding 0.

\emph{Against no control.} A policy set passes if its delay is at least 10\,\% lower with the interval excluding 0 and its close-following is not harmed under the harm rule above \ledger{V459}.
Without the second part, a delay gain bought with more close-following would count as a win.
Confirmatory~3's no-control clause is reported beside it: no control's close-following, delay and braking, and, where at least 4 of 5 policies are clearly worse on close-following, the sentence ``no control had fewer such seconds, at [y]\,\% more delay''.

\emph{Outcomes.} Each cell receives one of three pre-written sentences:
W, a T2 win in a cell where a 3E rule counts;
G, a win against the grid only, with no counting 3E rule beaten;
N, no T2 win where a 3E rule counts.
A cell where no 3E rule counts does not close the claim against tuned rules (the protocol's rule D10, \ledger{V454}).

\emph{Stop rules.} Confirmatory~3 carried an early KILL: the chain stopped if no rule beat no control on delay by at least 10\,\% with the interval excluding 0 in X1 or X2 at 5,300\,veh/h. In round~2 the floor KILL above removes single candidates.

\subsection{Integrity rules}\label{sec:integrity}

Every run uses a frozen copy of its commit, and a replay at that commit must reproduce the stored rows \ledger{V398}.
Traced runs must equal untraced runs on every numeric key, because a trace once changed a run \ledger{V386}.
If a bug is found after results, it is fixed, everything affected is re-run, and both outcomes are logged.
A gate statistic gets a no-effect null before it is used, after a gate was found that could not fail \ledger{V244}; such nulls exist for confirmatory~3's reading, the collapse check and T1's path (b), but not yet for the 10\,\% no-control bar, the 2\,\% counting filter, the side limits or the tile rule.
What a counter measures is traced on a sample before it enters a claim \ledger{V283}; this is done for two counters only (L20).
Rewards are smoke-tested in the mode in which training runs \ledger{V294}, the policy inputs were audited \ledger{V387--V389}, and every policy set passes a collapse check.
Adversarial reviews were run before the decisive runs \ledger{V377, V453}; the lighter reviews are disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}.

\subsection{The map-tile rule}\label{sec:tile_rule}

The decision map of Section~\ref{sec:results} shows one tile per cell (X1 or X2 at 5,300\,veh/h), measure and opponent, computed on the policy arm (the per-seed mean over 5 policies) with a 95\,\% percentile bootstrap and the sign convention above \ledger{V462}.
A tile is W if close-following is at least 10\,\% better (delay: at least 5\,\%) with the interval's lower bound above 0; L if close-following is more than 10\,\% worse (delay: more than 2\,\%) by point with the interval's upper bound below 0; and T otherwise.
Flow, braking at 3\,m/s$^2$, full-emergency events and collisions appear as markers where they breach T2's side limits, and the number of single policies meeting W is shown beside each tile.
The rule was logged after round~2's validation rows existed and before any test row.
No tile is a verdict: only T2 and the no-control bar decide.
