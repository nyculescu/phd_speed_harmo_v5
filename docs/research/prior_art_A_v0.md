## How this was verified

- Every source was checked by the verifier, independently of the two searchers.
  - Metadata comes from the arXiv API, Crossref, PMLR, the GitHub API, publisher or portal pages and TRID.
  - Claims were checked by keyword search of the full texts the searchers downloaded, after confirming each file's title page, or of the abstract pages.
- Status labels:
  - **verified**: exists, and the claim is supported at the stated level;
  - **verified, corrected**: exists and the core claim holds, but a detail is corrected;
  - **metadata-wrong**: the claim exists under a different source; the correct source is given;
  - **exists, claim unsupported**: the work exists, but the claim was not found at the level read;
  - **not-found**: dropped (see the appendix).
- Read-level labels: [FT] full text read by a searcher; [FT-kw] full text, keyword or section search only; [ABS] abstract; [SNIP] search snippet only; [CODE] repository files.
- The verifier's spot checks are keyword-level. A searcher's "full text" claim is not a full re-read by the verifier.

## 6. Implications for the design (proposals for the author, not decisions)

1. **The gap still looks open.** The question is the drop size as an axis, with tuned classical controllers and RL compared along it. Cite Ke et al. 2021 (C1) as the closest precedent and Jin & Jin 2015 (C2) as the analytical prior. Write "not found in this search" and close V153 G0 with WoS/Scopus before any novelty wording.
2. **∆ = 0 is the negative control.** Jin & Jin find zero mainline-control gain at ∆ = 0. Per V244, compute the drop estimator and every gate statistic's no-effect null on the ∆ = 0 plant with the same estimator. Set the thresholds against that null before any control run.
3. **Isolate the drop mechanism.** Gains can also come from off-ramp blocking by spillback (Gomes et al. 2008), from postponing stochastic breakdown (Zhang & Levinson 2010, abstract) and from ramp-storage limits. Design the synthetic geometry with no off-ramp upstream of the bottleneck, or make off-ramp blocking its own pre-declared axis. Report breakdown probability separately from ∆.
4. **Standing-queue drop and jam-wave discharge are different quantities** (Han et al. 2026: up to 30 % for jam waves, against a 3-18 % field range for standing queues). Define A's axis as the standing-queue drop at a fixed bottleneck, measured from cumulative counts, and report the jam-wave discharge separately.
5. **Candidate drop knobs.** Each knob-to-∆ map is fitted on calibration seeds only and frozen before any control result (V245):
   - (a) an explicit ∆ in a CTM/LWR plant, which is cheap but has no CAV microphysics;
   - (b) hesitation or acceleration delay (Han et al. 2026, with an analytical QDF check). SUMO `startupDelay` is only a partial match: it acts after a full stop only;
   - (c) Krauss sigma or zipper settings (V139).

   A candidate ladder, the searcher's suggestion for the author: about {0, 3, 7, 12, 18} % for standing queues, plus one 25-30 % jam-wave-like rung.
6. **Opponent fairness across ∆.**
   - Retune ALINEA / PI-ALINEA per ∆ level.
   - Any MPC must model the hysteresis (Cao et al. 2021), or it is a straw man.
   - Report both a per-∆-tuned and a fixed-tuned classical arm, so that the adaptation gain and headroom H have the right reference.
7. **CAV share moves the effective drop** (Wei et al. 2026; rs-10046871 [SNIP]). Fix ∆ on human-only traffic and report the measured ∆ and QDF at each penetration as predictions. Treat penetration as a second axis.
8. **The Vinitsky reproduction is a separate plant variant (BN4-V23).**
   - Use the fork geometry (edge 2 = 410 m) and the stored params.json driver set: IDM 2.6 / 4.5 / 1.0 / minGap 2.5, step 0.5 s.
   - Log the paper-text conflict (Krauss against IDM).
   - Measure its ∆ with A's estimator, so that it sits on the ladder as a labelled rung. The ≈ 33 % is only an inference.
   - The 2018 Lagrangian plant (≈ 800 against 1,000 veh/h, IDM a 1.0 / b 1.5) is the one our BN4-Flow copies. Do not conflate the two.
9. **Compute.** The stored runs took 46.9-95.0 h each for 2 M environment steps, so retraining at the paper's scale is infeasible. Replay the stored checkpoints first, then run a reduced, pre-registered retraining. Report the paper's best-of-35 seed rule only as "as published"; claims use the median over seeds, with checkpoints chosen on validation seeds.
10. **Metric bridge.** The paper measures only outflow at a fixed inflow above capacity, where the origin queue grows without bound. A's operating map needs a peaked demand profile for door-to-door delay, while G1 keeps the paper's protocol.
11. **For B (pointer only):**
    - Cassidy & Rudjanakanoknad 2005 (abstract) locate the merge drop in the shoulder lane, spreading laterally through lane changes. B's "does it bind" check could track shoulder-lane discharge and lane-change counts.
    - TORBO (Laval & Xu 2020) is a possible strong classical reference. Its gain figure stays [VERIFY], because the sources conflict.
