DRL against rules: where, who wins, by how much

Confirmatory 3, revision 4 (V395-V398), and its fresh-seed replication, confirmatory 3b (V401). r2m3, plant_c3, 50 % ALKS. 5 per-CAV PPO policies (d0-d4) against no control, the fixed 0.8 s rule (p333) and tuned rules. 20 paired seeds per cell. Companion pages: handoff, DRL course.

1. The verdicts

X1, slowdown downstream of merge 2

Not won (V395)

3 of 5 policies clear. Only the 0.8 s rule counted. 33-46 % fewer TTC seconds than it, but 2 policies had 2.5-3.1 % more delay than allowed.

X2, slowdown between the merges

Won at the minimum (V395)

4 of 5 clear against 4 rules: 24-30 % fewer TTC seconds than the strongest rule, delay within 2 %. Fragile: about 30 % on resampled seeds.

X2, 20 fresh seeds

Not confirmed (V401)

1 of 5 clear. All 5 still had 14-26 % fewer TTC seconds than the strongest rule, but 4 of 5 had 1.6-10 % more delay.

Against no control

Mixed on TTC, always faster

In the deciding X1 and X2 cells no TTC gain (X1: 31 % more TTC seconds). In S, H1 and H2 at 5,300: 47-56 % fewer. Delay always 18-37 % lower.

How to read every number here. For both measures, less is better. 'fewer' close-following seconds and 'less' delay mean the policy is better; 'more' means it is worse. Blue marks the policy better, orange marks it worse. Close-following (TTC) counts vehicle-seconds where a vehicle would hit the one ahead within 3 s at current speeds. It is a safety surrogate and is also in the policies' reward. Delay counts all vehicles' lost time. Percentages are the protocol's statistic: the median over the 20 seeds of the paired difference, relative to the other controller. 'Clearly' means the 97.5 % interval lies entirely on that side.

2. Where: every scenario

Each box is the median over the 5 policies; hover for each policy with its interval. 'Better' or 'worse' means the interval excludes 0 that way. The map rows (not primary) compare with the 0.8 s rule and no control only; the tuned rules ran only in the primary rows at 5,300. Blue: policies better. Orange: policies worse.

MeasuredPattern. Against the fixed 0.8 s rule, the policies clearly cut TTC seconds only in the slowdown cells at 5,300: 38-42 %, all 5 policies, at about equal delay. Elsewhere the medians favour them (14-39 % fewer) but at most 3 of 5 are clearly fewer, and in steady traffic at 4,600 there is no difference. Against no control the picture splits. At 5,300 in steady traffic, H1 and H2, and in X1 at 4,600, the policies have 47-56 % fewer TTC seconds, because no control queues. In X1 at 5,300 they have 31 % more, and in S at 4,600 about twice as many, on tiny counts. In X2 they are about equal. They always have 18-37 % less delay than no control.

Dot: median; line: 97.5 % interval. Blue: the policy passes that condition; orange: it fails. Dashed red: the pass line; right of it passes. A cell is won when at least 4 of 5 policies meet every condition against every counting rule, plus flow, braking and collision limits not drawn here.

5. What it adds up to

Holds in every test

Fewer seconds at TTC below 3 s than every tuned headway rule in X2, on both seed blocks: 8.5-30 % (original), 14-26 % (fresh), and 29-45 % fewer than the 0.8 s rule. Human drivers benefit too (V398).

Does not hold

A clean win: on fresh seeds the policies needed 1.6-10 % more delay than the best delay-efficient rule. X1 failed on delay too. In the deciding cells they do not beat no control on TTC.

Where it comes from

The queue left after a slowdown at the near-saturated second merge. Not during the slowdown itself, and not clearly in steady traffic (V396).

Honest one-line summary

Per-CAV PPO headway control traded a little delay for fewer close-following seconds than tuned rules after a slowdown on this uncalibrated two-merge model; the pre-registered win did not replicate.
