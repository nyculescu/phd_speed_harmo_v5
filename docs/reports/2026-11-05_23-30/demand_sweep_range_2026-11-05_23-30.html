Demand sweep: the training range for round 1

2026-11-05, V416-V419. Plant r2m3 (plant_c3), training slowdowns, constant demand 2,500-7,500 veh/h, 10 seeds (7,440,600-609), 1,680 runs, 0 failed. Medians of 10 paired seeds; "less" and "fewer" mean better than no control. Code 6d3a1a6.

What it shows

  • Pre-registered readings (V416 range rule and V418 impact band): both give 2,500-6,250 veh/h, with no edges.
    • The lower end is the bottom of the grid: every controller has at least 10 % less delay than no control even at 2,500, so the sweep never found where impact stops.
    • The upper end is set by served demand: the best arm serves 91 % at 6,250 and 88 % at 6,500. No arm had a teleport or a collision above the limit.
  • Delay does not mark a band. Gaps of 0.8 s cut delay against no control's 1.6 s at every level, by 11-40 %. That holds for both rules and all the policies, so it is a property of the headway, not of DRL.
  • Close-following marks a band.
    • The policies (median of 5) have fewer close-following seconds than no control only at 5,000-5,500.
    • p333 and os1_15_10 have fewer only at 5,000 (os1_15_10 also at 5,250).
    • Below 5,000, no control's counts are tiny (0-59 seconds per run) and every controller has more.
    • From 5,750 up, the policies are about level with no control, while the rules have more.
  • Post-hoc reading, not pre-registered: a close-following band of about 5,000-5,500, with edges at 4,500-4,750 and 5,750-6,000, so a training range of about 4,500-6,000. This matches the earlier exploratory band (V409).
