Options memo: improving the SAR, the DRL algorithm, and the search

"Fewer" and "less" mean DRL is better; "more" means worse. "Close-following" means TTC below 3 s, read once a second. "The rule" means the best tuned rule os1_15_10. Literature not read in full is marked [VERIFY].

In short

The biggest finding is about how the results are judged, not about the controller. Delay is the side of the bar that fails. The 2 % delay limit is judged on the point estimate, and it sits inside the 20-seed noise. On fresh seeds, each policy's delay interval against the rule is 7 to 12 points wide. Pooled over both 20-seed blocks (40 seeds, exploratory, so confirmatory 3b's verdict stands), all 5 policies have 15-25 % fewer close-following seconds, every interval clear of zero. Their delay ranges from 2.5 % less to 3.5 % more, and every interval includes zero. So any candidate judged on 20 seeds gets a near coin-flip verdict on delay.

Next, the agents found three measured weaknesses that a change can target:

  • Delay is priced right in total but not per vehicle. The reward's total weights match the bar's rate. But one vehicle sees other vehicles' delay at about 1/200 of that rate, through a shared term it can hardly move.
  • Late training slides toward close-following. After about update 100, the training reward is flat and the weights wander. Between update 250 and update 500 the policies traded delay for fewer close-following seconds (5 seeds, too few to settle).
  • About half of the decisions cannot act, and they still train the policy. The V2V gate is closed, and the PPO loss has no mask.

Recommendation for all three paths together: one cheap check with no training, then one paired round of up to 4 candidates, one from each path plus the gate mask, judged on 40 seeds.

F2. Training stops improving early, then slides toward close-following

  • The reward is flat: flat after about update 100, with every slope over updates 100-499 within noise (p2a, p3c).
  • The weights wander: from u50 to u500, the straight-line distance over the path length is 0.37 in all 5 learners. A random walk gives about 0.33 and a directed path 1.0 (p2a).
  • The final policy differs from u250 (5 tuning seeds): fewer close-following seconds in 9-10 of 10 learner-cells, and more delay in 7-8 of 10 (p2a, p2b, p3c). Five seeds cannot settle whether early checkpoints mislead (p3c).
  • The training reward cannot rank learners. Its last window ranks them opposite to their 40-seed evaluation, with a rank correlation of minus 0.7 over 5 learners (p3c).
  • The trust region never binds: KL is 0.0018-0.0026 and the clip fraction 1.2-1.5 %. The learning rate (3e-4) and the entropy weight (0.01) are constant (p2a).

F3. About half the decisions cannot act, and they still train the policy

  • The gate is often closed. It is open in 48-52 % of ALKS-seconds in training (p3a) and 56-57 % in evaluation (p1c).
  • Closed decisions still enter the loss. They go into the PPO loss without a mask: collect adds every due vehicle and update has no mask (c_train.py:155-243, checked). That is about 33,000 rows per update with no immediate effect (p1b).
  • They still carry advantage. Gated decisions have about 70 % of the advantage size of decisions that act (p3a). When gated, the policies stay near random: entropy 1.09-1.24 against 0.64-0.75 when open, where uniform is 1.39 (p3a).
  • Chatter: 51-58 % of level changes reverse the previous one (p1c, p2c, p3a).
  • Action aliasing. With the one-step rate limit, the 4 levels give at most 3 distinct moves. The greedy evaluation picks a different move than the most probable one in 9.5-24 % of decisions (p1c).

F4. The reward prices delay right in total, but not per vehicle

  • Own delay. The own-speed term is exactly the vehicle's own share of the judged delay. One own close-following flag weighs as much as 20 own vehicle-seconds of delay. The bar's own rate is 19-23 vehicle-seconds per close-following second, so the two match (p1a).
  • Others' delay reaches a vehicle only through the shared count term, at 1/419 per vehicle-second. For that vehicle, one close-following second weighs as much as about 4,200 vehicle-seconds of others' delay, about 200 times the bar's rate (p1a).
  • Where the variance goes. The shared terms carry 11 % or less of the per-decision reward variance, but 27-51 % of the advantage variance (p1b, p3a).
  • The safety flag is sparse. It is set in 6.5-9 % of decisions, yet carries 76 % of the per-vehicle variance (p1b, p3a).
  • Shorter gaps do not buy delay. p333 (0.8 s everywhere) has about the same delay as the rule. On fresh seeds, ramp-1 delay is 2.8-10.7 % more than the rule's for all 5 policies (p1a).

F5. The policies leave the rule mostly at merge 2, which no input describes

  • Where they differ. At least 3 of 5 learners choose a different level from the rule in 87-89 % of decisions on road 3 and 4a. Upstream and at merge 1 it is 25-30 % (p1c, on trace states from an older policy).
  • Inputs that do not describe merge 2:
    • input [1] is at its limit in 93 % of decisions;
    • [13] is a merge-1 zone speed only;
    • [11] is dead, and its weights never trained (p1c).
  • Effects there, on fresh seeds: 124-232 fewer close-following seconds on road 3. Merge-2 lane-end stops range from 454 fewer to 106 more (p1c).

F6. Compute

  • Where training time goes: about 95 % to environment collection; the PPO update takes about 4 % (p2c, micro-benchmark).
  • The idle CPU is not free. CPU sits at 38 % because 20 processes run on 32 threads, and extra workers slowed training before (V392).
  • Unit costs: one candidate (5 learners) fills one 3.3-h slot. Evaluation runs about 1,500 runs per hour (p3c).

Path 1: improve the state, action and reward (PPO unchanged)

1A. Attributed delay replaces the spacing payment recommended

Problem
F1, F4: delay fails, and others' delay is priced about 200 times too low per vehicle. The flow term pays for short spacing, which buys no delay (p333).
Change
Reward only, in _net_stats (b_env.py:754-791):
  • each vehicle's lost time, 1 − min(v/limit, 1), is charged to its ALKS leader, the way follower TTC already is;
  • an accelerating-lane vehicle's lost time is charged to the nearest mainline ALKS within 50 m;
  • the reward becomes −0.5 × (own + followers' + merging vehicles' lost time) − 10 × safety, and the flow weight goes to 0.
The weight 0.5 equals the own-speed weight, the bar's rate, and is fixed before data. State, action and PPO are unchanged.
Compute
About 4.5 h: smoke check in training mode 0.3, training 3.3, evaluation about 1. Build about half a day.
Risk
  • The attribution is not causal: a bottleneck queue is charged to whichever ALKS leads it.
  • Pricing slow followers may shorten gaps and spend the close-following margin.
  • It partly double-counts the speed-around term.
Test
Arms n0-n4, d0-d4, os1_15_10, p333 and no control, in X2 and X1 at 5,300, on fresh seeds. KILL if fewer than 4 of 5 pass the bar in X2, or if the median delay against the rule is not at least 2 points less than baseline-v3's on the same seeds (p1a).

1B. Gate mask: hold the level, train only on decisions that can act

Problem
F3: about 43 % of policy-gradient rows have no immediate effect. With the gate closed, the flow term changes by only 0.001-0.003 whatever the move (p1b).
Change
  • _apply (b_env.py:652) holds a vehicle's level while its gate is closed.
  • The gate flag travels with each row through _obs, _act and collect.
  • update (c_train.py:213-243) averages the policy and entropy terms over gate-open rows only. The value loss and GAE keep every row.
The reward is unchanged.
Compute
About 4 h. Build 2-3 h.
Risk
It loses the deferred setting: a closed decision now sets the level that acts once the gate opens. It may not touch delay.
Test
e0-e4 against d0-d4. The reward is identical, so the training curves compare directly too. KILL if neither close-following nor delay improves against d0-d4, or if delay is more with the interval clear of zero.

1C. Merge-2 inputs and a three-move action

Problem
F5: the information gap at merge 2. F3: aliased actions.
Change
  • The dead input [11] becomes the position relative to merge 2.
  • Two inputs are added: the merge-2 zone speed and the 4a_0 count, taking the input count from 16 to 18. The inputs the rules read are unchanged.
  • The action becomes {longer, keep, shorter}, which the rate limit already implies.
Compute
About 3.6 h. Build about 40 lines.
Risk
  • The policy may already infer merge 2 from inputs [0] and [14].
  • It may sharpen close-following at 4a while delay still fails.
  • It makes two changes in one arm.
Test
KILL if no more learners pass than baseline-v3's on the same seeds, or if the new inputs show about zero permutation importance.

1D. Safety signal at step resolution, so the judged sample leaves the reward next round

Problem
Teaching to the test. On fresh seeds, ALKS hard braking counted every 0.1 s is up to 23 % more than under no control, and only 5-15 % fewer than under the rule (p1a).
Change
In _second (b_env.py:397-404), accumulate dt × [braking ≥ 3 m/s²] and dt × max(0, 1/TTC − 1/3 s) every step, for each ALKS and its follower. These replace the 1-Hz flags.
Compute
About 5-6 h. Training may run 1.2-1.5 times slower; this is not measured.
Risk
It punishes close gaps harder, so gaps get longer and delay grows: the side that already fails.

Path 1 recommendation: 1A

Pros:

  • It aims at the side of the bar that fails.
  • It uses the bar's own quantity at the bar's own rate, with no tuned weight.
  • It is one reward change, and costs about 4.5 compute hours.

Cons:

  • The attribution is a heuristic.
  • The judged 1-Hz close-following flag stays in the reward.
  • It may spend some of the close-following margin.

Inferior alternatives:

  • 1B isolates credit cleanly but does not aim at delay. It is a strong round candidate, not the path-1 pick.
  • 1C aims where close-following is won, and its link to delay is indirect. It also makes two changes in one arm.
  • 1D is right for credibility but pushes delay the wrong way. It fits after delay has margin.
  • A local follower-speed term in place of the shared terms removes the only system-delay signal, including the insertion queues (p1b).
  • The follower charge only for human followers targets a smaller, unmeasured component.
  • 400-m or 1-km look-ahead: the only evidence for it is a rule that bought road-3 safety with more delay (p1c).
  • A lower TTC weight spends the margin on shorter gaps, and p333 shows short gaps carry no delay edge.
  • A higher weight on the shared terms keeps per-vehicle credit at about 1/250 and adds noise.
  • Changing the start level or the rate limit: neither is shown to bind (p1c).
  • Speed or lane actions were already declined or had no headroom (V309, V339, V344).

Path 2: improve the DRL algorithm, including adjusting the SAR

2A. Choose checkpoints on validation seeds, from existing checkpoints step 0, no training

Problem
F2: the final policy is not chosen, and the drift went toward more delay.
Change
None to the SAR or PPO. Evaluate u50 to u500 of all 5 learners greedily, with os1_15_10, p333 and no control, on 20 validation seeds in X2 and X1 at 5,300. Pick with a rule fixed before data: the fewest close-following seconds with delay at most 2 % above the rule's.
Compute
About 1.4 h (about 2,100 runs). A small selection script.
Risk
A winner's curse on near-ties, and overfitting to X2. It spends validation seeds.
Test
Selected s0-s4 against final d0-d4 on fresh test seeds. KILL if they pass in no more policy-cells, or if they are not less delayed with close-following no worse. The same runs also answer path 3's ranking question and 2C's step-0 stop.

2B. Anneal, and give every learner the same critic scale

Problem
F2: a constant learning rate and entropy weight. The return scale was fixed at update 0, between 2.84 and 4.00, so the value weight differs about 1.7-fold between learners. C2 has the largest scale and is the weakest learner (n = 5, suggestive only; p2a).
Change
  • Learning rate linear to 0.
  • Entropy weight from 0.01 to 0 over the second half.
  • One critic scale for all learners.
  • TensorBoard scalars for the policy loss, gradient norms and per-term reward means.
Compute
About 3.6 h. Build half a day.
Risk
It freezes the wandering wherever it stands, so delay may not move. With entropy at 0, the flow term may push every car to 0.8 s.
Test
KILL if no more policy-cells pass than d0-d4's and the learner spread is not smaller.

2C. PPO-Lagrangian: safety in the reward, delay as a constraint recommended

Problem
F1, F2, F4: fixed weights put the trade-off in the wrong place, and late training drifts further toward close-following.
Change
  • Reward: the safety terms, collisions and the move charge. The speed and flow terms leave the reward.
  • A cost channel: the delay rate of the vehicle itself, of its 200-m bin and of the network. Training also computes the judged delay.
  • A cost critic head on the same 26 inputs, and its own GAE. The two advantages are combined as (A_r − λ·A_c)/(1 + λ).
  • The dual variable λ is updated every update from the last 20 updates' delay relative to a reference. The target is 3 % less than the reference, as a margin for noise.
  • The reference is the lower-delay of os1_15_10 and p333, under training demand, on reference seeds. It never comes from X1, X2 or evaluation seeds.
Family: PPO-Lagrangian [VERIFY].
Compute
About 4.5 h, including step 0 (about 0.5 h). Build about 1 day.
Risk
  • The per-vehicle cost is only a proxy for episode delay.
  • λ may hit its cap, or oscillate on the 6 % episode-to-episode noise.
  • A constraint met under training demand may not hold in X2.
  • Falling back to 0.8 s everywhere meets the constraint but loses the edge.
Step-0 stop
If 4 of 5 baseline-v3 policies already meet the target under training demand, the constraint would not bind: do not train. The fresh-seed result would then be a transfer or noise problem.
Test
KILL if any of these holds:
  • fewer than 4 of 5 policies pass in X2;
  • the paired delay is not less than baseline-v3's;
  • λ sits at its cap for the last 100 updates in 3 of 5 learners.

2D. Diagnose the critic offline, then a richer critic only if the data justify one

Problem
Explained variance stays at about 0.6. Nobody can yet tell whether the critic lacks capacity, lacks information, or faces irreducible noise (p2c).
Change
  • Dump 30 training-mode episodes on throw-away seeds.
  • Fit 4 critics offline and compare them with a shuffled null: the current one; a 256-256 one; one with the previous decision added; one with the 6 nearest vehicles added.
Compute
Under 0.3 h, plus 3.3-3.8 h only if a richer critic is then trained.
Risk
A better fit of the returns need not give a better policy. The dump is an exception to TensorBoard-only logging (V394).

Path 2 recommendation: 2A as step 0, then 2C

Pros:

  • 2A costs about 1.4 h with no trainer build, tests F2 directly, and is the same run as path 3's stage 0.
  • 2C has the bar's form: the most safety subject to a delay limit. One adaptive number replaces a weight search, and its step 0 can stop it within about half an hour.

Cons:

  • 2C is the largest build in this memo, about a day.
  • The per-vehicle cost is only a proxy for episode delay.
  • It overlaps 1A, since both put delay into the objective. In the same round, that makes them a fair pair: a fixed weight against an adaptive one.

Inferior alternatives:

  • 2B is hygiene. It freezes the drift instead of steering it, and is better added later to whichever candidate wins.
  • 2D is useful diagnosis, but no measured gap points at the critic, and the update takes only about 4 % of the time.
  • The reverse constraint (delay in the reward, close-following as the constraint) constrains the surrogate itself, so it caps the claim and comes closer to teaching to the test.
  • Fixed safety weights of 5 and 2.5 cost twice the compute for an end point that drifts; 2C automates that search.
  • A larger batch or more environments per update: Adam's step barely depends on batch size, and contention slowed training before (V392).
  • More updates, a larger learning rate, more epochs: the clip is idle and the curve flat, so these add wandering, not progress.
  • An attention or graph policy: 1.5-2 days of build, with no direct measured gap, and not before 2D.
  • A recurrent policy: no measured memory failure (V410, V411).
  • A wider actor or a GPU: no measured gap, and the update is about 4 % of the time.

Path 3: search for a better DRL algorithm, including framing a SAR

3A. The search procedure: one calibration stage, then a fixed-budget paired round recommended

Problem
  • F1: noise decides at 20 seeds.
  • F2: the training reward cannot rank or stop runs.
  • Learner spread is as large as the effects sought. At 40 seeds the learners range from 15 to 25 % fewer close-following seconds, and from 2.5 % less delay to 3.5 % more (p3c).
Procedure
  • Stage 0 = 2A. It also decides whether early stopping is worth building. If the best learner at u500 is not in the top 2 at u250, or the rank correlation is below 0.8 in a cell, early-stopping rungs are dropped.
  • Round: at most 4 candidates. Each trains 5 learners for 500 updates in one 3.3-h slot, on the same 5 training seed streams, so comparisons are paired. Only the existing collapse check stops a run.
  • Validation: 40 seeds, X1 and X2 at 5,300, each candidate at u250 and u500, with the incumbent and the rules. At most 2 candidates advance. To advance, a candidate needs less delay than the incumbent (interval clear of zero) and at least 10 % fewer close-following seconds than the rule.
  • Test: 40 fresh seeds, used once, read with confirmatory 3's tuned reading. Bonferroni over finalists × cells: 98.75 % intervals for 2 × 2.
Compute
About 17 h for 4 candidates (training 13.2, validation 2.6, test 1), plus 1.4 h for stage 0.
Risk
  • Even 40 test seeds leave about 3-6 points of noise on delay against a 2 % limit.
  • Shared seeds share their quirks.
  • 4 candidates is a cap, not a search of the whole space.
Round KILL
No candidate beats the incumbent on delay on validation: baseline-v3 stays, and path 3 stops for this round.

3B. Residual PPO on the tuned rule path 3's candidate

Problem
  • DRL wins close-following, but its delay spreads by about 10 % from seed to seed, while the rule already holds the delay (F1).
  • The rule needs no new inputs: OwnState reads inputs [0], [4] and [15] (p3b).
Change
  • Action: an offset around the rule's level for that vehicle. The hook exists at b_env.py:656-657 as a static per-zone residual.
  • State: the 16 inputs plus the rule's level.
  • Network: the output layer starts with offset 0 at about 0.9 probability, so training starts as the rule.
  • Reward: unchanged.
Compute
About 4.2 h; small build. Smoke check: with the offset forced to 0, the run must reproduce the stored os1_15_10 row exactly.
Risk
  • The same reward may teach the same deviations.
  • From 0.8 s, an offset of ±1 cannot reach 1.2 or 1.6 s; ±2 can (a protocol detail).
  • A win is only as strong as os1_15_10.
  • An earlier residual around a static base on r1m3 was mixed (V317).
Test
KILL if fewer than 4 of 5 pass in X2, or if the residual's delay is not less than baseline-v3's.

3C. COMA-style counterfactual advantage in the current PPO

Problem
F3: gated decisions carry advantage although their action does nothing.
Change
A Q critic with 4 outputs; the advantage becomes Q(a) minus the policy-weighted mean of Q [VERIFY: Foerster et al.].
Compute
About 3.8 h. Build half a day.
Risk
The Q differences may sit below the critic's error. Benchmark reports put COMA below MAPPO [VERIFY]. And 1B attacks the same problem more cheaply.

3D. Shared Double-Dueling DQN with n-step returns

Problem
  • The evaluation is greedy anyway.
  • The safety events are sparse, and replay can reuse them.
  • When the gate is closed, a dueling advantage head can learn that no action matters.
Compute
About 5.5-7 h, including a disclosed 1.4-h tuning pilot. Build 1-1.5 days.
Risk
  • The replay data keep shifting as 150-270 neighbours change policy.
  • The −10 safety spikes can cause overestimation.
  • It needs more tuning than PPO [VERIFY].

3E. CMA-ES over a per-zone own-state rule opponent upgrade, before any claim

Problem
The 70-rule grid is coarse: its front reaches 15-17 % fewer close-following seconds at 2-3 % more delay than os1_15_10 (p3b). Part of DRL's edge may come from a weak grid.
Compute
About 4-8 h.
Risk
It overfits the tuning seeds. The result is a tuned non-learning controller: a stronger opponent, not a candidate.

Path 3 recommendation: 3A, with 3B in path 3's slot; 3E before any thesis claim

Pros:

  • The procedure answers measured problems: noise, a reward that cannot rank, and learner spread.
  • Selection happens on validation, so a multiplicity correction is needed only at the test.
  • The incumbent needs no retraining.
  • 3B starts at the rule's delay with a readable deviation, at the same compute as baseline-v3.

Cons:

  • It needs 40 + 40 new evaluation seeds and about a day per round.
  • 4 candidates is a cap.
  • 3B is only as strong as os1_15_10.

Inferior alternatives:

  • Successive halving as the main procedure: rank preservation is not shown, it needs an exact resume, and it saves at most about 1.6 h per candidate, since 5 learners already share one slot.
  • One learner per candidate: the learner spread is as large as the effects sought.
  • Screening on the training reward: its rank correlation with the evaluation is minus 0.7.
  • 20 test seeds: about 5-8 points of noise on delay.
  • 3C: 1B tests the same problem more cheaply.
  • 3D: the largest build, and a tuning confound.
  • Imitation of the rule, then fine-tuning: the policy may drift back under the same reward. It is a diagnostic, not a candidate.
  • A supervisory zone agent: it failed twice here (V279, V285), and it leaves per-CAV control.
  • VDN or QMIX: the shared part is 11 % or less of the variance, and QMIX needs a fixed set of agents.
  • SAC-discrete: like 3D, plus a temperature to tune.
  • Independent learners or mean-field methods: they address no measured problem, or only the smallest measured term.
  • ES over network weights: the fitness is too noisy.
  • Model-based RL: a vehicle's own headway barely changes its own speed (V289), so the model must resolve tiny effects.

Decisions for the author

  1. Round-1 candidates (at most 4). I recommend 1A, 1B, 2C and 3B: one reward change, one credit change, one algorithm change and one prior-knowledge family.
    • Pros: each aims at a different measured weakness, and all share seeds and the incumbent.
    • Cons: about 13 h of training and about 2-3 days of build.
    • Inferior: 1C instead of 1B puts two changes in one arm. Fewer candidates save 3.3 h each, but lose the side-by-side reading.
  2. Evaluation size. I recommend 40 seeds per cell for validation and for the test, with the 2 % delay limit unchanged in this round.
    • Pros: this narrows the delay noise from about 5-8 points to about 3-6.
    • Cons: twice the evaluation runs, about 1-2 h more.
    • Inferior: 20 seeds leave delay near a coin flip (F1). Restating the limit now, after a failure, would look like moving the bar; that belongs to the thesis-bar discussion (V95, V402).
  3. Checkpoint rule. CLAUDE.md says both "final policy only" and "checkpoints chosen on validation seeds only". I recommend that a checkpoint chosen on validation may be the reported policy, under a selection rule fixed before data, with the final policy also reported.
  4. Seeds. Stage 0, round training, validation and test each need fresh, approved ranges. I will propose exact ranges in the protocol after checking the ledger and the run folders. One agent suggested ranges (source/p3c.md); I have not checked them.
  5. 2C's safety signal. I recommend keeping the judged 1-Hz flag in round 1, so that 2C differs from baseline-v3 in the algorithm only. The step-resolution signal (1D) comes after.
    • Con: teaching to the test stays for one more round. It is offset by reporting the counters that are not in the reward: TTC below 1.5 s, the severe check, braking counted every 0.1 s.

After your pick: the protocol for the round, criteria fixed before data. Suggested effort Extra High, ultracode on for its design review, then High for builds and smoke checks.

Method and caveats

  • Agents: 9 headless agents, 3 per path, ran as systemd jobs from the brief in source/agent_brief.md. Their full answers are in source/p1a.md … source/p3c.md, and their scripts in /home/catalin/work/phd/v5b1_runs/sar3c/tmp/. They read data and code only: no SUMO, no training, no seeds, no edits to the repo.
  • Checks I made: F1 (source/main_noise.py, output beside it) and the missing loss mask (c_train.py:155-243 at d2b2ecc). The other numbers are the agents', with their sources named.
  • Older trace data: per-decision numbers in F3, F4 and F5 come from an older phase-3 policy on one or two used seeds, with the hand-scaled inputs. They are indicative, not measurements of d0-d4.
